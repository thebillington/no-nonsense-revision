Try the kind of questions you will meet

Six questions from across the course. Open a question to reveal the answer and the explanation behind it.

Storage, service and food pairing · Sweetness and umami How can sweet food make wine seem? Show answer
  1. Lower in alcohol
  2. Less acidic and sweeter
  3. More fruity and less bitter
  4. More drying and bitterCorrect

Why: Sweet food makes wine seem more drying and bitter, more acidic, less sweet and less fruity. A dessert with a light, crisp wine is the classic mismatch.

Sparkling and fortified wines · Traditional method What happens during the second fermentation in the traditional method? Show answer
  1. It ferments again in the bottle, trapping carbon dioxide as pressureCorrect
  2. The wine is boiled to drive off the alcohol before it is bottled
  3. The grapes are pressed again and the juice ferments a second time
  4. The wine is filtered and the yeast is added back in before bottling

Why: In the traditional method, yeast and sugar are added to a base wine that has already fermented once, and a second fermentation follows in the bottle. The carbon dioxide is trapped and becomes the fizz.

Principal grape varieties · Cabernet Sauvignon Which description fits Cabernet Sauvignon? Show answer
  1. Medium acidity, medium tannin, medium body
  2. High acidity, low tannin, light body
  3. High acidity, high tannin, medium to full bodyCorrect
  4. Low acidity, low tannin, full body

Why: Cabernet Sauvignon is typically high in acidity, has high tannin and a medium to full body. It is deeply coloured.

Regionally important grape varieties · Tempranillo Which aromas are typical of Tempranillo? Show answer
  1. Lychee and rose
  2. Red and black fruit with leatherCorrect
  3. Green apple and lemon
  4. Green pepper and asparagus

Why: Typical aromas of Tempranillo are strawberry and red cherry; blackberry and black plum; dried fruit, leather and mushroom.

Winemaking and bottle aging · Alcoholic fermentation What does yeast convert grape sugar into during alcoholic fermentation? Show answer
  1. Alcohol and carbon dioxideCorrect
  2. Water and oxygen
  3. Oak and lees
  4. Tannin and acid

Why: Yeast converts the sugar in the grape juice into alcohol and carbon dioxide. The alcohol stays in the wine, and in a still wine the carbon dioxide escapes.

Environmental influences and grape growing · Climate and wine style Which characteristics are typical of a wine from a cool climate? Show answer
  1. Higher acidity, lighter body, lower alcoholCorrect
  2. Higher tannin, fuller body, higher alcohol
  3. Lower acidity, lighter body, higher alcohol
  4. Lower acidity, fuller body, higher alcohol

Why: A cool climate has a temperature of 16.5°C or below, and the wines are lower in alcohol, lighter in body, lower in tannin and higher in acidity.
